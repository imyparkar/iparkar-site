Certification or certificate?

Choose by what you need to demonstrate, not just the name on the award.

Professional certification and taught qualifications compared
What to compareProfessional certificationCertificate or diploma course
PurposeValidates professional knowledge and competence against a credentialing body's standard.Develops and assesses learning against a course or qualification syllabus.
Typical entryMay require a degree, relevant work experience and an existing credential.Depends on the programme; some have no formal entry requirements.
AssessmentUsually a certification examination and review of eligibility evidence.May include exams, assignments, practical tasks or attendance requirements.
Keeping it currentOften involves renewal fees, continuing development and recertification.The award usually records achievement at a point in time; provider rules vary.
ExamplesASP, CSP, CIH, CFPS and QEP.NEBOSH International General Certificate, IOSH Level 3 Certificate and Level 6 diplomas.

A certificate of attendance is different from an assessed qualification. NEBOSH and IOSH qualifications assess learning; a course does not automatically award a professional certification. EPI is an interim designation towards QEP, not a certification. Always check the awarding body's terminology and recognition.

ASP Associate Safety Professional®

BCSP · checked 24 Oct 2026

Associate Safety Professional® (ASP)

The foundation BCSP credential for safety practitioners — and a qualifying credential on the road to the CSP.

Official page

Requirements

  1. At least a bachelor’s degree in any field, or an associate degree in safety, health or environment (with at least four SH&E courses — 12 semester / 18 quarter hours — in ASP blueprint domains).
  2. One year of safety, health and environment experience, at least half of it preventive, professional-level work with breadth and depth of safety duties.
  3. Pass the ASP examination.
  4. Keep the credential active: annual renewal fee plus recertification points every five years.
  5. Degrees from outside the United States are evaluated for U.S. equivalency during the application.
  6. You must disclose any criminal convictions or actions against a professional licence or credential.

Application process

  1. Create an account in BCSP’s “My Profile” portal and start a new application from the Applications menu.
  2. Submit the application and pay the application fee; BCSP then reviews your eligibility.
  3. Once approved, you have one year to take and pass the exam.
  4. Buy the exam (US$350 for a single attempt at the time of checking — see BCSP’s Credentials At-A-Glance for current fees), either bundled with the application or afterwards.
  5. Schedule through the single sign-on link to Pearson VUE and sit the computer-based exam at a test centre. Results are available as soon as you submit.
  6. If you need a retake, the next attempt must be at least six weeks after the last one.

Exam preparation

  1. Download the ASP exam blueprint and build your study plan around its domain weightings.
  2. Work through the ASP reference list — focus on calculations, toxicology, ergonomics and management systems.
  3. Practise timed, mixed-topic questions until you consistently score well above your target.
  4. Use the free 10-question sample here, then the full premium bank for timed mock exams.

CSP Certified Safety Professional®

BCSP · checked 24 Oct 2026

Certified Safety Professional® (CSP)

BCSP’s flagship credential for safety leaders who run safety management systems and shape culture.

Official page

Requirements

  1. At least a bachelor’s degree.
  2. Four years of safety, health and environment experience, at least half of it preventive, professional-level work with breadth and depth of safety duties.
  3. A BCSP qualified credential — for example ASP, GSP, TSP, CIH, CMIOSH/CFIOSH, CRSP, NEBOSH National or International Diploma, or an AICTE-recognised Diploma/Certificate in Industrial Safety (India). See BCSP’s page for the full list.
  4. Pass the CSP examination.
  5. Keep the credential active: annual renewal fee plus recertification points every five years.
  6. Degrees from outside the United States are evaluated for U.S. equivalency; all education requirements must be met.

Application process

  1. Log in to BCSP’s “My Profile” portal and start a CSP application from the Applications menu.
  2. Upload evidence of your degree, experience and qualified credential, then pay the application fee.
  3. Once approved, you have one year to take and pass the exam.
  4. Buy the exam (US$350 for a single attempt at the time of checking — confirm current fees on BCSP’s At-A-Glance).
  5. Schedule with Pearson VUE through single sign-on and sit the computer-based exam; results are immediate.
  6. Retakes must be at least six weeks after your previous attempt.

Exam preparation

  1. Map your study plan to the CSP blueprint domains and their weightings.
  2. Expect scenario-based questions on safety management systems, risk assessment and leadership — not just recall.
  3. Refresh the maths: rates, statistics, ventilation, noise and engineering economics.
  4. Finish with full-length timed mocks to build exam stamina.

CFPS Certified Fire Protection Specialist®

NFPA · checked 24 Oct 2026

Certified Fire Protection Specialist® (CFPS)

NFPA’s credential for professionals who reduce fire loss through applied fire protection knowledge.

Official page

Requirements

  1. Eligibility combines education and fire-protection work experience. The exact routes are set out in NFPA’s CFPS Candidate Handbook — read it before you apply.

Application process

  1. The application steps, fees and exam rules are set out in the CFPS Candidate Handbook.
  2. Applications are made online through NFPA’s certification portal.

Exam preparation

  1. Follow NFPA’s official CFPS learning path.
  2. The exam is built on the NFPA Fire Protection Handbook — know its structure well.
  3. Practise mixed questions across fire science, detection, suppression and life safety.

CIH Certified Industrial Hygienist®

BGC (Board for Global EHS Credentialing) · checked 24 Oct 2026

Certified Industrial Hygienist® (CIH)

The leading credential for industrial hygiene and occupational and environmental health and safety (OEHS) professionals who anticipate, recognise, evaluate and control workplace health hazards.

Official page

Requirements

  1. A bachelor’s or higher degree from an accredited institution, including 60 semester hours in science, maths, engineering or science-based technology — at least 15 of them at junior, senior or graduate level.
  2. Industrial hygiene coursework: 180 academic hours or 240 continuing-education contact hours, at least half covering IH/OEHS fundamentals, toxicology, measurements and controls.
  3. At least two contact hours of ethics coursework.
  4. Four years (48 months) of professional-level industrial hygiene experience, and active practice at the time you apply. An ABET-accredited IH degree can reduce this by up to one year.
  5. Agree to the BGC Code of Ethics and Ethics Case Procedures.
  6. Keep the credential active with an annual maintenance fee and BGC’s certification maintenance (recertification) process.

Application process

  1. Read the CIH Candidate Handbook in full before you start.
  2. Create an account in the BGC Portal and submit your application: transcripts (unofficial copies are accepted at first), coursework, and a description of your IH experience.
  3. Degrees from outside the U.S. or Canada need a credential evaluation.
  4. Pay the application fee (US$160 at the time of checking).
  5. Once approved, pay the exam fee (US$370 at the time of checking) and schedule at a Pearson VUE test centre.
  6. Exams run in two windows a year — spring (April–May, apply by 1 February) and fall (October–November, apply by 1 August). Approval stays active for four windows (two years).
  7. The exam has 180 multiple-choice questions (150 scored) in two 2.5-hour sections, with an on-screen calculator.

Exam preparation

  1. Study across all the tested areas: air sampling and instrumentation, toxicology, ventilation and engineering controls, noise, radiation, thermal stress, ergonomics, biohazards, biostatistics and epidemiology, and IH program management.
  2. Drill the calculations — ventilation, sampling volumes, noise, radiation and exposure averaging — until they’re fast and reliable.
  3. Practise with the on-screen calculator only; personal calculators aren’t allowed.
  4. Book your exam window early and plan around the 1 February / 1 August deadlines.

QEP Qualified Environmental Professional

BGC (Board for Global EHS Credentialing) · checked 24 Oct 2026

Qualified Environmental Professional (QEP)

An international, multi-media, multidisciplinary credential for environmental professionals. The exams contain no country-specific regulations.

Official page

Requirements

  1. Route 1: a bachelor’s degree in environmental, physical, earth or natural sciences, engineering or mathematics, plus at least five years of professional environmental experience. An EHAC-accredited environmental master’s counts for one year of experience.
  2. Or a bachelor’s degree in any discipline plus at least eight years of professional environmental experience.
  3. Route 2 (under five years’ experience): earn the EPI designation first, then complete the QEP once you have five years of experience.
  4. Three professional references from environmental professionals (supervisors, co-workers, clients).
  5. Agree to the BGC Code of Ethics and Ethics Case Procedures.

Application process

  1. Submit the QEP application form (there is a separate form if you already hold the CIH).
  2. Arrange for at least three environmental professionals to complete the Professional Reference Questionnaire — it’s your job to make sure all three arrive.
  3. Pass the General Environmental Science (GES) exam and the QEP exam. EPI holders don’t retake the GES.
  4. Pay fees by the due date — see the QEP Candidate Handbook for current amounts and exam details.

Exam preparation

  1. Download the 2024 GES and QEP exam blueprints from the official page and map your study plan to them.
  2. Expect broad, multi-media coverage — air, water, waste, remediation, environmental management systems — rather than local law.
  3. If you hold the CIH, check the CIH-holder route in the handbook.

EPI Environmental Professional In-training

BGC (Board for Global EHS Credentialing) · checked 24 Oct 2026

Environmental Professional In-training (EPI)

An in-training designation for final-year students and early-career environmental professionals, valid for up to seven years on the way to the QEP.

Note EPI is an interim designation, not a credential. It is a stepping stone for students and early-career professionals working towards the QEP.

Official page

Requirements

  1. Be in the final (senior) year of a bachelor’s programme in environmental, physical, earth or natural sciences, engineering or mathematics — or an early-career professional who already holds such a degree.
  2. At least three references who know your academic work, such as professors or course instructors.
  3. Pass the General Environmental Science (GES) exam.

Application process

  1. Confirm you meet the education requirement.
  2. Submit the EPI application form.
  3. Have at least three referees complete the Professional Reference Questionnaire.
  4. Pass the GES exam — passing awards the EPI designation.
  5. Within seven years, gain five years of professional environmental experience and pass the QEP exam to become a QEP (no GES retake needed).

Exam preparation

  1. Use the GES exam blueprint on the official QEP page as your syllabus.
  2. Revise core environmental science from your degree — chemistry, biology, earth science and basic engineering maths.
